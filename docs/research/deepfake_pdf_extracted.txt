Abstract
Reliable deepfake image detection under lossy compression and generator shift remains
challenging. This study describes an image-level binary classifier combining an ImageNet-
pretrained Xception spatial branch with a differentiable three-level Daubechies-4 wavelet-
packet decomposition (WPD) branch, a learnable high-frequency enhancement (HiFE) module,
and bidirectional dual cross-attention (DCA) fusion. The image-level experiment uses a
140,000-image real-versus-synthetic face collection identified as FFHQ versus StyleGAN2,
partitioned into 100,000 training, 20,000 validation, and 20,000 test images with balanced
classes. The reported ROC-AUC point estimates are 0.937 at JPEG quality 50 and 0.888
at JPEG quality 23. Implementation testing reports 13 successful architecture tests and six
successful differentiable-WPD tests on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU using PyTorch
2.6.0 and CUDA 12.4. Matched per-variant ablation metrics, repeated-seed uncertainty
estimates, numeric cross-generator results, and native FaceForensics++ video results are
not reported here. The findings therefore characterize initial JPEG behavior on one image
collection and do not establish independent gains from WPD, HiFE, or DCA, or generalization
to video domains.
Keywords:deepfake detection; digital image forensics; wavelet-packet decomposition; JPEG
robustness; spatial-frequency fusion; cross-generator evaluation; reproducibility.
1 Introduction
The increasing realism of manipulated and synthesized facial media creates challenges for digital
trust, identity verification, and forensic analysis. Deep-learning detectors have been developed
to identify such content from spatial appearance, frequency statistics, temporal inconsistencies,
and combinations of these cues. FaceForensics++ established a widely used benchmark for
manipulated facial videos and highlighted the importance of evaluating detectors under different
compression conditions [1]. Xception-based convolutional detectors are also common baselines
for facial manipulation detection, in part because their depthwise-separable convolutions provide
expressive spatial features at a practical computational cost [2].
A key challenge is that performance on a held-out split from one dataset does not necessarily
imply robustness to changes in generator, source domain, codec, or post-processing. Frequency-
domain methods exploit artifacts that may be difficult to identify from RGB appearance alone.
Franket al.investigated frequency analysis for recognizing GAN-generated images, while Qianet
al.introduced a frequency-aware framework for face forgery detection [3,4]. However, frequency
features can also encode generator-specific or dataset-specific shortcuts; recent work on frequency
debiasing explicitly examines this concern [5].
Wavelet-packet decomposition has previously been studied for deepfake image analysis [6];
wavelet-packet filter-bank methods are grounded in multiresolution and best-basis analysis [7,8].
Later research has explored content-guided spatial-frequency reasoning, adaptive fusion, and
high-frequency enhancement for compressed content [9–11]. Therefore, this paper does not claim

The central research question is:
Under matched data splits and optimization budgets, does the proposed WPD-enhanced
spatio-frequency detector improve detection under compression and domain shift
beyond the benefits attributable to compression-aware augmentation alone?
The study is organized around five testable hypotheses:
• H1:Compression-aware augmentation improves detection under the tested degradation
conditions.
• H2:WPD contributes an incremental benefit over a spatial-only baseline when training
conditions are matched.
•H3:HiFE contributes measurable benefit beyond the same model without enhancement.
• H4:DCA provides measurable benefit over simple concatenation under a comparable training
protocol.
• H5:Any observed in-domain improvements persist, at least partly, under external-dataset or
unseen-generator evaluation.
These hypotheses are empirical. None is considered supported until the corresponding
experiment has been run and its uncertainty reported.
The paper makes the following intended contributions. First, it specifies a reproducible dual-
branch design in which spatial appearance and wavelet-packet features are processed separately
and then fused. Second, it defines matched component ablations to isolate the effects of WPD,
HiFE, DCA, and compression-aware augmentation. Third, it establishes an evaluation protocol
that separates native FaceForensics++ video compression from additional JPEG transformations
and includes external-domain testing. Fourth, it requires reproducible data manifests, repeated
seeds, calibrated operating points, and test procedures whose outputs are derived from actual
experimental runs.
The completed empirical scope is limited to the 140k real/fake face collection identified as
FFHQ versus StyleGAN2 and evaluated under JPEG image recompression. Two quantitative
AUC values are available; the GAN-to-diffusion transfer experiment is described only qualitatively
and lacks sample counts, generator details, and uncertainty estimates. Native FaceForensics++
video evaluation and complete component-wise ablations remain outstanding. The attention
module operates at the spatial-frequency fusion stage rather than applying attention directly to
individual wavelet packets.
2 Related Work
2.1 Spatial-Domain Detection
Spatial detectors learn discriminative patterns from RGB pixels. Xception uses depthwise-
separable convolutions to model spatial and channel relationships efficiently [2]. The Face-
2

Forensics++ benchmark provides a common setting for evaluating facial manipulation detectors
under different compression conditions [1]. While spatial representations are strong baselines,
their results should be interpreted in the context of the dataset, manipulation methods, and
compression pipeline used for evaluation. Generalization must be measured rather than inferred
from within-dataset performance.
2.2 Frequency-Domain Forensics
Frequency representations can reveal irregularities associated with image synthesis and upsam-
pling. Franket al.studied frequency analysis for deep fake image recognition [3]. Qianet al.
proposed F3-Net, which combines frequency-aware decomposed image components with local
frequency statistics [4]. These studies demonstrate that frequency information is an established
direction in forgery analysis; consequently, using a frequency transform alone is not a sufficient
novelty claim.
2.3 Wavelets and Wavelet-Packet Decomposition
Wolteret al.introduced wavelet-packet analysis for deepfake image detection and examined
its use for spatial-frequency forensic features [6]. A conventional discrete wavelet transform
recursively decomposes the low-frequency approximation branch, while a wavelet-packet trans-
form recursively decomposes low- and high-frequency nodes. This provides a more complete
multiresolution partition of the signal, but the usefulness of a representation still depends on the
task, implementation, and evaluation protocol. This work investigates a differentiable db4 WPD
implementation as a component of a larger detector; it does not claim to introduce WPD for
deepfake detection.
2.4 Spatial-Frequency Fusion and High-Frequency Enhancement
Research has explored interaction between spatial content and frequency clues. Wanget al.
proposed content-guided spatial-frequency relation reasoning with dynamic graph learning [9].
Wanget al.later investigated adaptive fusion of spatial-frequency features [10]. Gaoet al.studied
a high-frequency enhancement network for highly compressed deepfake content [11]. These works
overlap conceptually with the motivation for combining spatial features, frequency information,
and learnable feature weighting. The present study therefore needs controlled experiments to
establish what, if anything, is added by its particular combination of WPD, HiFE, and DCA.
2.5 Generalization and In-the-Wild Evaluation
FreqDebias identifies the risk that deepfake detectors may over-rely on selected frequency bands
associated with particular forgery types, motivating explicit analysis of spectral bias and cross-
domain performance [5]. The 2026 WGN work also investigates wavelet-guided spatial attention,
compression, and external-dataset generalization [12]. Deepfake-Eval-2024 provides a recent
in-the-wild benchmark assembled from media circulated in 2024, illustrating the importance
of moving beyond academic test conditions [13]. These studies raise the bar for a convincing
evaluation: testing only one real-image source, one synthetic generator, or a few quality settings
is insufficient for broad deployment claims.

2.6 Research Gap
Prior work has separately established the relevance of spatial detectors, frequency-based cues,
WPD, feature fusion, and high-frequency enhancement. The remaining question for this project
is narrower: whether the selected combination provides benefits that remain after controlling for
augmentation, training budget, and data distribution. This is particularly important because a
full model trained with more data or more optimization steps cannot be fairly compared with
a smaller baseline trained under a weaker protocol. The study therefore prioritizes matched
ablations, dense compression evaluation, external-domain testing, and uncertainty estimates over
a claim of novelty based only on the architecture.

4 Proposed Method
4.1 Overview
The proposed framework contains two branches. The spatial stream encodes RGB appearance
using Xception features [2]. The frequency stream converts the image to luminance, applies a
three-level db4 WPD, and processes the resulting packet maps with a learnable enhancement
block. A dual cross-attention module then allows the two streams to exchange information before
binary classification. Fig. 1 summarizes the data flow.
4.2 Face Acquisition and Preprocessing
When the source is a video, frames are extracted using a documented and fixed sampling
policy. Face regions are then detected using a documented face detector such as MTCNN or
RetinaFace. The specified preprocessing pipeline uses a 20% bounding-box margin to retain
blending boundaries, five-landmark alignment when available, and bilinear resizing to224× 224
pixels. If alignment fails, the fallback policy and failure rate must be reported; frames must not
be silently dropped in a class-dependent manner.

5.2 Partitioning and Leakage Prevention
Split video/source identities before extracting frames. No source video or near-duplicate frame
should appear in more than one partition. Perform exact-hash checks and perceptual-hash checks
where appropriate. Audit whether real and fake classes differ in metadata, resolution, crop
distribution, encoding, or background in ways that could become shortcuts. Generate train,
validation, and test manifests once, version them, and record their hashes in every experiment
log.
5.3 Compression Protocol
The completed image-level evaluation reported in the available experiment record uses JPEG
quality factors Q∈{ 100, 50, 23} and a broader sweepQ∈{ 100, 90, 75, 50, 30, 20, 10}. The
reported quantitative points currently available are for JPEGQ = 50and Q = 23; the quality-10

recompression and must remain distinct from FaceForensics++ video compression labels C0,
C23, and C40. The native FF++ video evaluation was not completed in the reported workspace

6.3 Ablation and Cross-Generator Evidence
The six canonical architecture variants are implemented in the repository, but the available
experiment record does not contain their full per-variant metrics under a matched protocol.
Consequently, no ranking among the spatial-only, WPD-only, concatenation, HiFE, DCA, and
full-model variants is reported here. In particular, the available information does not establish
that DCA outperforms simple concatenation or that every added component contributes an
independent gain.
A qualitative drop is reported when transferring from GAN-generated content to diffusion-
generated content. However, the transfer dataset counts, generator list, numeric AUC, and
uncertainty estimates are not specified, so the magnitude of this domain shift cannot be quantified
from the current results. Cross-generator robustness therefore remains an open evaluation
question.
6.4 Planned External Evaluation
No FaceForensics++ video files were available in the evaluated workspace, so no FF++
C0/C23/C40 metrics are reported. Celeb-DF v2 and DFDC remain external evaluation targets
pending audited runs. Because the network is a binary classifier without a segmentation decoder,
no pixel-level localization scores are reported; Grad-CAM and attention maps are qualitative
feature-inspection tools only.
7 Discussion
The reported JPEG AUCs provide an initial measurement of image-level performance on the
FFHQ-versus-StyleGAN2 collection: AUC is 0.937 at Q = 50and 0.888 at Q = 23. The
decline is consistent with increasing degradation, but the limited reported points and absence of
uncertainty statistics limit the strength of any robustness claim. In particular, JPEG quality
factors must not be relabelled as FF++ C23/C40, and image-level results should not be presented
as video-benchmark results.
The reported architecture and WPD tests passed on an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU using PyTorch
2.6.0+cu124 and CUDA 12.4. These tests support the stated tensor shapes and differentiable
implementation. They do not show that the full model is more accurate than the ablations, or
that DCA prevents frequency-branch undertraining. Such conclusions require matched training
runs, per-variant metrics, uncertainty estimates, and appropriate statistical tests.
The study defines five hypotheses covering augmentation (H1), incremental WPD contribution
(H2), HiFE contribution (H3), DCA versus concatenation (H4), and domain-shift performance

architecture is an image-level binary classifier and does not provide pixel-level manipulation
masks. JPEG recompression also cannot represent every social-media or video-transcoding
pathway.
8 Conclusion
This paper documents a dual-branch deepfake image classifier built from an ImageNet-pretrained
Xception spatial stream, a differentiable three-level db4 wavelet-packet branch, a learnable
high-frequency enhancement module, and bidirectional dual cross-attention fusion. According to
the implementation record, the WPD and architecture test suites passed on an NVIDIA RTX
4060 Laptop GPU under PyTorch 2.6.0+cu124 and CUDA 12.4. On the reported FFHQ-versus-
StyleGAN2 image evaluation, ROC-AUC point estimates were 0.937 at JPEG quality 50 and
0.888 at JPEG quality 23; a decline at quality 10 was reported without a supplied numeric
value. These findings are specific to the stated image dataset and JPEG protocol. Complete
matched-ablation metrics, repeated-seed uncertainty, and fully quantified external-generator
results are still needed before claiming that the proposed architecture components independently
improve compression robustness or generalization.
