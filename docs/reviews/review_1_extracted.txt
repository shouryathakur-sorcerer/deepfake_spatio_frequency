
Journal-Paper Restructuring and Publication-Readiness Review
Executive assessment
Editorial decision

Decision on the manuscript in its present form: major revision / reject-and-resubmit before journal submission. I would not recommend acceptance in its current state.

The project has a potentially publishable core: a dual-branch detector combining an Xception spatial stream with a differentiable two-level Daubechies-4 wavelet-packet stream, followed by adaptive feature fusion. The full-scale model reports excellent in-domain performance on FFHQ versus StyleGAN2, including 99.52% accuracy and ROC-AUC 0.99995 at JPEG Q=100 and 97.94% accuracy with ROC-AUC 0.99904 at Q=23. The manuscript also reports useful calibration measures, confusion matrices, ablations, and a GPU implementation speed improvement.

However, the main scientific problem is not poor performance; it is insufficient evidence that the proposed architecture itself causes the reported compression robustness. The headline model was trained with 100,000 images for eight epochs and on-the-fly JPEG augmentation, whereas the architectural baselines were trained with only 5,000 images for two epochs and without JPEG augmentation. Therefore, the experiments currently confound architecture, dataset size, optimization budget, precision mode, and augmentation. 

A second major issue is novelty. Wavelet-packet deepfake analysis was already explicitly developed by Wolter et al. in 2022; that work found that WPD preserves spatial information while providing frequency localization and that fused wavelet/CNN architectures can outperform or compete with DCT and raw-image approaches. Spatial-frequency fusion, adaptive frequency extraction, multiscale attention, and adaptive wavelet/spatial fusion have also appeared subsequently. [1] The manuscript therefore cannot safely claim that WPD-based deepfake detection, spatial-frequency fusion, or adaptive fusion itself is the research novelty.

At the same time, the broader research problem remains important. Recent reviews continue to identify compression, data-distribution shifts, and generalization as major weaknesses in deepfake detection, while recent primary work increasingly evaluates unseen generators, cross-dataset performance, and real-world or online-social-network degradation. [2] A 2026 in-the-wild benchmark reported a 45% AUC decrease for open-source image detectors relative to earlier academic benchmarks, emphasizing how misleading near-perfect in-domain results can be. [3]

The gaps that genuinely survive the literature review

The manuscript should not center its novelty on “frequency-domain deepfake detection,” “wavelets,” “WPD,” or “spatial-frequency fusion.” Those areas already have substantial prior art. [15]

The defensible research gaps are narrower:

First, it remains valuable to establish whether full wavelet-packet decomposition contributes robustness independently of compression augmentation, rather than simply showing high performance when both are used.

Second, the relative behavior of WPD, DWT, DCT and FFT under controlled, severe, and varying JPEG degradation can be studied more rigorously. Recent work's use of dense compression sweeps means three isolated quality factors are no longer a sufficiently comprehensive characterization. [10]

Third, whether WPD-derived cues survive generator shift from StyleGAN2 to diffusion and other synthesis families is an important unanswered question for this particular architecture. Modern deepfake research increasingly treats unseen-generator performance as a central requirement. [16]

Fourth, the interaction among frequency representation, JPEG augmentation, fusion design, and calibration is potentially publishable because the manuscript already exposes an interesting negative result: in the 5k/no-augmentation experiment, spatial-only accuracy at Q=23 is 87.06%, higher than ParallelSE's 84.69% and CrossGated's 82.59%.This suggests that a frequency branch does not automatically confer compression robustness; it may require compression-aware training. That is a scientifically interesting finding and should be elevated rather than hidden.

Fifth, the GPU-native differentiable WPD implementation may form a legitimate reproducibility/efficiency contribution if its numerical equivalence, latency, memory use, and training throughput are rigorously demonstrated.

The literature therefore supports a revised novelty statement along these lines:

We do not introduce wavelet packets or spatial-frequency fusion for deepfake detection. Instead, we investigate whether a GPU-native differentiable full wavelet-packet representation provides measurable complementary robustness to a strong spatial detector under controlled JPEG degradation, quantify its dependence on compression-aware training, and analyze how adaptive fusion affects calibration and operating-point behavior.

That claim is narrower, but much more credible.

Editor-style quality review and restructuring
Manuscript structure

The current document has the components of a technical report, but a journal paper requires a tighter question → method → controlled evidence → generalization → limitation chain. The present paper mixes implementation achievements, full-scale results, short-budget ablations and SOTA comparisons in a way that makes causal interpretation difficult. 

I recommend this final structure:

Title

Compression-Robust Deepfake Image Detection with Differentiable Wavelet-Packet Features and Adaptive Spatio-Frequency Fusion

Abstract

Use five logical elements in approximately one paragraph: operational problem; precise gap; proposed method; fair experimental protocol; principal results. Do not lead with near-perfect FFHQ–StyleGAN2 numbers alone. The final abstract should include one in-domain robustness result and one unseen-generator/cross-dataset result once available.

Introduction

Start from the deployment problem, not from broad claims about generative AI. Then establish three evidence-backed gaps:

deepfake detectors degrade under compression and distribution shift;spatial-frequency and wavelet methods already exist, but the incremental value of full WPD under controlled compression-aware training remains insufficiently isolated;robustness must be validated beyond a single generator and synthetic JPEG setting.

Recent work makes generalization central, including LSDA, FreqDebias and D³. [11]

End the introduction with testable hypotheses, not only contributions:

H1: compression-aware training improves both spatial and spatio-frequency models.H2: WPD provides an incremental robustness gain after controlling for augmentation.H3: adaptive fusion improves threshold-independent discrimination and/or calibration beyond simple concatenation.H4: the WPD-enhanced model preserves part of this advantage under unseen-generator and cross-dataset shifts.

Related Work

Organize around: spatial detectors; global/local frequency methods; wavelet/WPD methods; spatial-frequency fusion; compression robustness; generalization. The literature review must explicitly acknowledge Wolter et al. 2022, dynamic spatial-frequency reasoning in 2023, adaptive wavelet/spatial fusion in 2024, FreqDebias and D³ in 2025, and recent wavelet-guided robustness work in 2026. [19]

Method

Use a single architecture figure containing the data flow from RGB image → spatial branch/WPD branch → packet representation → fusion → classifier. Add a second small schematic showing two-level WPD decomposition. The method should state tensor dimensions after every stage.

Experimental Protocol

Make this a major section, not a few paragraphs. Include datasets and provenance, train/validation/test counts, identity independence, duplicate checks, preprocessing, codec implementation, exact JPEG quality scheme, augmentation probability, seeds, optimizer, stopping/selection rule, threshold-selection policy and statistical tests.

Results

Use this sequence:

controlled component ablation;

JPEG robustness curve;

generator/domain generalization;

calibration and operating points;

efficiency;

qualitative/failure analysis.

This ordering answers causal questions before displaying headline performance.

Discussion

Discuss why frequency information helps, where it fails, and especially the current negative finding that an unaugmented spatial-only model can outperform dual-branch models in raw Q=23 accuracy. fileciteturn0file0 Tie this to contemporary concerns about spectral shortcuts: frequency cues can themselves overfit to training-specific artifacts. [12]

Limitations and Reproducibility

Keep explicit limitations and add generator coverage, data provenance, platform simulation limits, demographic/source-domain limitations and computational cost.

Conclusion

Do not say the experiments “establish wavelet-packet attention as a robust paradigm.” A journal-appropriate conclusion would state exactly what was supported by the matched experiments.

operational evidence: robust performance across compression, generator, and domain shift;

deployment: precision/recall operating points, calibration, latency, throughput and memory;

reproducibility: code, model and evaluation manifests.

The existing precision-versus-recall comparison between CrossGated and ParallelSE is particularly useful for this applied framing, once thresholds are selected properly on validation data. 
A journal I would not prioritize in the current form

A seemingly natural choice would be Forensic Science International: Digital Investigation, but its official scope is unusually restrictive for generic image-forgery detectors: it states that general techniques for detecting forgery in digital photos/videos are outside scope unless they are tied convincingly to actual digital-investigation settings and suitable representative datasets. For this reason, I would not make it one of the first three targets unless the study gains genuine investigative-case evaluation. [29]

Recommended work allocation

The ML lead should own the WPD verification, matched ablations, generator-transfer study and efficiency benchmarking.

The data/reproducibility owner should create immutable split manifests, audit identities/duplicates/source metadata, package environments and verify end-to-end reproducibility.

The statistics owner should specify the analysis before the final experiment runs, especially seed aggregation, confidence intervals, ROC-AUC comparisons, calibration and threshold selection.

The lead/corresponding author should own literature integrity, novelty framing, manuscript rewriting, journal alignment, ethics/declarations and the cover letter.

A senior author who was not responsible for day-to-day implementation should conduct the final adversarial review. That person should attempt to reject the paper on novelty, leakage, experimental fairness and unsupported claims before an external editor has the opportunity to do so.
Timeline



Final acceptance gate

I would consider the paper ready for journal submission only after the following five conditions are simultaneously met:

Scientific novelty gate: The authors no longer claim novelty for wavelet packets or spatial-frequency fusion in general; the exact incremental contribution is distinguished from Wolter et al. and subsequent adaptive fusion/wavelet work. [30]

Causal-evidence gate: The headline WPD/fusion gain survives a matched-budget experiment and can be separated from the effect of JPEG augmentation.

Generalization gate: The detector is evaluated against unseen generators and at least one genuinely independent dataset. This is essential given current evidence that in-domain deepfake accuracy can fail badly under distribution shift and in the wild. [31]

Integrity/statistics gate: No citation mismatch remains; no incompatible benchmark is presented as a direct comparison; thresholds are selected exclusively on validation data; major performance claims have uncertainty estimates.

Reproducibility gate: The WPD implementation, training configs, manifests, seeds, environment, model checkpoints and evaluation scripts are accessible and reproduce the reported tables. This aligns particularly well with TIFS's explicit reproducible-research expectations. [21]

The single most important conceptual change is to move from:

“Our WPD-fusion model achieves 97.94% at Q=23, therefore WPD is compression resilient.”

to:

“Under matched conditions, we quantify how much robustness comes from compression-aware augmentation, how much additional robustness is contributed by WPD, whether adaptive fusion adds further benefit, and whether those gains persist across unseen generators and independent data.”

That change turns the project from a strong engineering demonstration into a much more defensible journal study.
