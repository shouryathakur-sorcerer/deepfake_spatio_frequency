
Dual-Branch Spatio-Frequency Deepfake Detection via Multi-Scale Wavelet-Packet Decomposition and Adaptive High-Frequency Enhancement

Abstract— The proliferation of hyper-realistic facial manipulation algorithms powered by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Diffusion models presents grave risks to digital media integrity, biometrics, and public trust. While existing deep learning detectors achieve near-perfect within-dataset accuracy (>97% AUC on pristine images), their performance collapses under cross-dataset domain shifts (falling to 50%–65% AUC) and severe lossy video compression (e.g., H.264 quantization at c40), which discards high-frequency manipulation cues. Furthermore, conventional frequency-based forensics relying on global Discrete Fourier (DFT) or Cosine Transforms (DCT) suffer from a spatial localization vacuum, rendering them ineffective on localized manipulations such as facial reenactments. To resolve these interrelated vulnerabilities, this Interim Technical Report (ITR) presents a novel Dual-Branch Spatio-Frequency Forensic Architecture. The proposed system decouples forensic feature extraction into: (1) a deep spatial representation branch capturing color, boundary, and blending anomalies, and (2) a multi-scale wavelet-packet frequency branch utilizing orthogonal Daubechies-4 filter banks to generate a 64-sub-band decomposition tree that preserves spatial coordinates while isolating directional high-frequency traces. To combat compression degradation, a learnable High-Frequency Enhancement (HiFE) residual block adaptively reconstructs attenuated coefficients. Spatial and spectral features are merged through a Dual Cross-Attention (DCA) module and regularized via binary cross-entropy with label smoothing. Comprehensive validation protocols on FaceForensics++ (raw, c23, c40) and Celeb-DF v2 establish explicit measurable targets: achieving >88.0% AUC under c40 compression and >80.0% cross-dataset AUC, while maintaining a strict stability standard deviation below 0.025 across 5 deterministic seeds.
Index Terms— Deepfake detection, digital image forensics, multi-scale wavelet-packet decomposition, high-frequency enhancement, cross-dataset generalization, lossy compression resilience, dual cross-attention.
I. PROBLEM FORMULATION & RESEARCH OBJECTIVES
A. Motivation and Background
Digital facial manipulation techniques, broadly classified under the umbrella term 'deepfakes', have evolved from crude academic experiments into accessible, photorealistic synthesis pipelines. Leveraging advanced deep generative models—such as Generative Adversarial Networks (StyleGAN2, StarGAN, FSGAN), Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), and neural rendering frameworks (Face2Face, NeuralTextures)—malicious actors can convincingly alter identity, facial expressions, and spoken audio in digital media. The weaponization of this technology for political disinformation campaigns, non-consensual defamatory imagery, financial fraud, and identity spoofing presents an unprecedented crisis for public trust, evidence verification in legal contexts, and automated biometric security. Consequently, designing defensible, robust, and verifiable forensic countermeasures has become an urgent mandate in applied artificial intelligence.
B. Technical Research Gaps in Contemporary Literature
A rigorous synthesis of the state-of-the-art literature across 26 forensic studies reveals three fundamental architectural and theoretical bottlenecks that severely impair current deepfake detection systems:
1) The Spatial Generalization Deficit (Domain Overfitting)
The dominant deepfake detection paradigm utilizes deep convolutional neural networks (e.g., Xception, ResNet, EfficientNet) trained via binary cross-entropy on localized RGB frames. While these models report extraordinary classification scores (>98% AUC) when tested on intra-dataset test splits, empirical evaluations demonstrate that their accuracy plummets to near chance (50%–65% AUC) when deployed against unseen manipulation algorithms or new target identities (Lin et al., 2024; Kaddar et al., 2024). The root cause lies in the fact that supervised CNNs memorize low-level, dataset-specific RGB artifacts, color distribution shifts, and sensor noise patterns unique to a single generator, failing to learn invariant representations of manipulation.
2) The Localization Vacuum of Global Frequency Forensics
To overcome RGB color overfitting, researchers have increasingly investigated frequency-domain clues, exploiting the fact that up-sampling operations (e.g., transposed convolutions) leave periodic checkerboard artifacts across the spectrum (Frank et al., 2020; Dzanic et al., 2020). However, prevailing frequency detectors rely on global orthogonal transforms, specifically the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) and global Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) (Qian et al., 2020). While effective at capturing global periodic spikes, global transforms completely discard spatial coordinates. As a result, global spectral methods suffer from a severe 'localization vacuum'—they cannot pinpoint where a forgery occurs and fail when manipulations are confined to localized regions (such as lip-syncing or expression transfer) or when faces are partially occluded.
3) The Compression Collapse (Social Media Laundering)
In practical distribution channels (social media feeds, messaging apps, video streaming), digital video undergoes aggressive lossy compression (e.g., H.264/MPEG-4 or H.265/HEVC at high quantization rates such as c40). Codec quantization acts as an aggressive low-pass filter that selectively discards imperceptible high-frequency transform coefficients. Consequently, passive forensic models whose decision boundaries depend on subtle spatial blending borders or unenhanced high-frequency spectral traces experience catastrophic failure, with AUC scores collapsing from >95% to 60%–70% (Gao et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2022).
4) Lack of Reproducibility & Model Stability Benchmarking
As highlighted in the Q1 Journal Guidelines and documented by Almestekawy et al. (2024), forensic literature exhibits a pervasive reproducibility crisis. Most published models report isolated peak accuracy figures while omitting stability evaluations across random weight initializations, leading to massive variance in performance when independent researchers attempt replication.

PROBLEM SUMMARY: Core Research Problem: Contemporary detectors either overfit to spatial RGB textures (collapsing cross-dataset), discard spatial localization in global frequency transforms (DFT/DCT), or collapse entirely under lossy video compression (c40). A defensible detector must simultaneously preserve spatial coordinates, isolate high-frequency directional sub-bands, and adaptively reconstruct compressed coefficients.

C. Formal Problem Statement
Let an arbitrary suspect facial media asset be represented as a normalized digital image or extracted video frame tensor X in R^{H x W x C}, where H, W, and C denote the height, width, and color channels (C=3 for RGB), respectively. The input media may have undergone an unknown lossy compression transformation with quantization parameter Q_F in [0, 51]. The objective of the forensic classification system is to learn a parameterized mapping function F_theta: X -> y_hat in [0, 1] that estimates the posterior probability that X is manipulated (y_hat >= tau, where tau in (0, 1) is the operational decision threshold, typically set to 0.50), while maintaining robust generalizability across arbitrary manipulation generators G and resilience across severe quantization levels Q_F >= 40.
D. Measurable Research Objectives
To satisfy rigorous Q1 validation criteria, this thesis establishes four explicit, quantitatively measurable objectives:
• Objective 1 (Compression Resilience): Achieve an AUC exceeding 88.0% on FaceForensics++ under severe c40 (heavy H.264) compression, outperforming standard Xception baselines by at least 6.0% (Gao et al., 2024).
• Objective 2 (Cross-Dataset Generalizability): Attain an AUC exceeding 80.0% under strict cross-dataset evaluation on the Celeb-DF (v2) benchmark when trained exclusively on FaceForensics++ (HQ/c23), overcoming the domain-shift barrier (Kaddar et al., 2024).
• Objective 3 (Spatial-Spectral Localization): Conserve 2D spatial coordinate mapping within the frequency domain by generating a 64-band Wavelet-Packet tree, achieving pixel-level forgery localization with an Intersection-over-Union (IoU) > 0.65 on spliced regions.
• Objective 4 (Reproducibility & Stability Guarantee): Restrict the standard deviation of classification accuracy (sigma_Acc) to below 0.025 across five independent runs using deterministic Mersenne Twister seeds (s in {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}), satisfying Section 7 of the Q1 framing protocol.

III. PROPOSED DUAL-BRANCH FORENSIC METHODOLOGY
To resolve the dual bottlenecks of domain-shift collapse and compression degradation, the proposed architecture separates feature learning into two specialized processing pipelines—Spatial Representation and Multi-Scale Wavelet-Packet Frequency—unified through an attention-driven cross-fusion module.
A. Stage 1: Facial Acquisition, Alignment, and Normalization
Given an input image or video frame sequence, the face region is localized using Multi-Task Cascaded Convolutional Networks (MTCNN) or RetinaFace. To ensure that subtle blending boundaries, color discontinuities, and hair-forehead transition seams are captured, the facial bounding box is expanded by a 20% margin (scale factor 1.2x). The cropped facial region is geometrically aligned using five facial landmarks (two eyes, nose tip, two mouth corners) and bilinearly interpolated into a canonical spatial dimension:


X_0 = Normalize(BilinearCrop(X, BBox_expanded)) in R^{224 x 224 x 3}


(1)

where pixel intensities are normalized to the bounded range [-1.0, 1.0].
B. Stage 2A: Deep Spatial Convolutional Representation Branch
The spatial stream processes the three-channel RGB tensor X_0 through a modified Xception backbone. Traditional standard convolutions are replaced with depthwise separable convolutions to factorize spatial filtering from cross-channel correlation learning. This suppresses parameter bloat and prevents the network from memorizing facial identity features. The spatial feature representation is extracted at the exit flow module before final pooling:


F_spa = G_spa(X_0; Theta_spa) in R^{C_s x H' x W'}


(2)

where H' = 14, W' = 14, and C_s = 728 channels. F_spa preserves spatial context, edge discontinuities, and boundary blending artifacts.
C. Stage 2B: Multi-Scale Wavelet-Packet Frequency Branch
To capture generator up-sampling traces without losing spatial coordinate indices, the frequency stream applies a 2D Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (DWPT) directly to the normalized luminance channel Y_0 = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B.
1) Quadrature Mirror Filter Formulation: The signal is convolved with low-pass scaling filters h_L and high-pass wavelet filters h_H using Daubechies-4 (db4) orthogonal wavelets, yielding four directional sub-bands at each dyadic step:


f_LL = h_L (x) h_L (Approx),  f_LH = h_L (x) h_H (Horiz),  f_HL = h_H (x) h_L (Vert),  f_HH = h_H (x) h_H (Diag)


(3)

2) Full 3-Level Decomposition Tree: Unlike the standard Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), which only recursively decomposes the low-frequency LL node, the Wavelet Packet Transform recursively decomposes all low- and high-frequency nodes up to scale Q = 3. At termination, the tree produces 4^3 = 64 equal-bandwidth spatial sub-bands:


W_Q in R^{64 x (H/2^Q) x (W/2^Q)} = R^{64 x 28 x 28}


(4)

This formulation retains spatial grid coordinates (28 x 28) while providing an ultra-fine spectral decomposition that isolates high-frequency checkerboard artifacts across all directional orientations (Wolter et al., 2022).
D. Stage 2C: Adaptive Learnable High-Frequency Enhancement (HiFE) Module
Under heavy H.264 video compression (c40), the high-frequency sub-bands (LH, HL, HH) suffer severe coefficient attenuation. To restore these forensic cues, a learnable residual enhancement block is embedded into the wavelet branch. Directional sub-bands are passed through channel-attention gating and directional convolution blocks:


W_gate = sigma( MLP(AvgPool(W_Q)) + MLP(MaxPool(W_Q)) )


(5)



W_enh = W_Q (*) W_gate + F_conv(W_Q)


(6)

where (*) denotes element-wise Hadamard multiplication. The enhanced wavelet tensor is projected through a stride-2 separable convolution layer to match the spatial branch grid, producing the enhanced frequency feature map F_freq in R^{256 x 14 x 14}.
E. Stage 3: Dual Cross-Attention (DCA) Spatio-Frequency Fusion
Rather than utilizing naive concatenation, spatial and spectral features interact through a Dual Cross-Attention (DCA) module. F_spa and F_freq are linearly mapped into Query, Key, and Value spaces:


Q_spa = F_spa W_Q1,   K_freq = F_freq W_K1,   V_freq = F_freq W_V1


(7)



Q_freq = F_freq W_Q2,   K_spa = F_spa W_K2,   V_spa = F_spa W_V2


(8)

The cross-attention interactions are calculated as:


A_{S->F} = Softmax( Q_spa K_freq^T / sqrt(d_k) ) V_freq


(9)



A_{F->S} = Softmax( Q_freq K_spa^T / sqrt(d_k) ) V_spa


(10)

The attended features are concatenated with residual skip connections and bottleneck-projected:


F_fused = LayerNorm( Concat[A_{S->F}, A_{F->S}] W_P + F_spa ) in R^{984 x 14 x 14}


(11)

F. Stage 4: Classification Head & Optimization Objective
The fused feature tensor F_fused is condensed via Global Average Pooling (GAP) into a 984-dimensional vector z. Vector z passes through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) comprising a dense layer (512 units), Batch Normalization, ReLU, Dropout (p = 0.5) for structural regularization, and a final linear projection with Sigmoid activation estimating manipulation probability y_hat:


y_hat = sigma( W_c^T z + b_c ) in [0, 1]


(12)

The end-to-end framework is optimized using Binary Cross-Entropy with Label Smoothing (epsilon = 0.05):


L_total = - (1/N) sum_{i=1}^N [ y_i^* log(y_hat_i) + (1 - y_i^*) log(1 - y_hat_i) ]


(13)

where y_i^* = y_i(1 - epsilon) + 0.5*epsilon. Label smoothing prevents the model from assigning overconfident probabilities to known artifacts, boosting cross-dataset generalization.

VI. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP & REPRODUCIBILITY GUARANTEE
A. Dataset Partitioning & Benchmark Suites
The framework will be evaluated across three benchmark datasets representing diverse generation methods, resolutions, and quality regimes: (1) FaceForensics++ (FF++), comprising 1,000 video sequences manipulated via five distinct algorithms (Deepfakes, Face2Face, FaceSwap, NeuralTextures, FaceShifter) evaluated across three compression levels: raw (uncompressed), c23 (light H.264 compression), and c40 (heavy compression); (2) Celeb-DF (v2), containing 5,639 high-quality videos exhibiting minimal visual artifacts, serving as the primary cross-dataset generalization benchmark; and (3) Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC), providing unconstrained real-world media featuring complex lighting, pose variations, and camera motions.
B. Implementation Environment & Optimization Parameters
• Software & Hardware: PyTorch 2.2 with CUDA 12.1 and Torchvision on an dual-NVIDIA RTX GPU cluster (24GB VRAM per node).• Optimizer & Schedule: AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate eta = 1e-4, weight decay lambda = 1e-5), regulated via a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule decaying to 1e-6 over 50 epochs with a mini-batch size of 32.• Data Augmentations: Random horizontal flipping (p = 0.5), mild Gaussian blurring (sigma in [0.1, 1.2]), JPEG compression re-encoding (Q in [50, 95]), and random brightness-contrast adjustments (+/- 15%).
C. Deterministic Seeding & Stability Protocol
To satisfy Section 7 of the Q1 framing protocol ('Reproducibility: Keep the Evidence, Not Only the Final Paper') and address the stability concerns raised by Almestekawy et al. (2024), all pseudo-random number generators (PyTorch, NumPy, Python standard random, CUDA CUDNN backend) will be deterministically seeded using five distinct Mersenne Twister seeds: s in {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}. In accordance with best practices, all reported metrics will present the mean and standard deviation across these five independent runs. A run is verified only if sigma_Acc < 0.025.
D. Quantitative Evaluation Metrics
System effectiveness will be assessed using four standard forensic evaluation metrics: (1) Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC), reflecting classification threshold-agnostic performance; (2) Detection Accuracy (ACC) at optimal operating threshold tau = 0.50; (3) Equal Error Rate (EER), the operating point where False Acceptance Rate (FAR) equals False Rejection Rate (FRR); and (4) F1-Score, the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall on manipulated media samples.

Technical Risk Assessment and Fallback Procedures
• Risk 1 (High GPU Memory Consumption in 64-Sub-band Tree): If computing full 64 sub-bands causes GPU out-of-memory errors during DCA cross-attention, sub-bands will be grouped into 16 directional macro-clusters via average pooling before cross-attention, reducing attention matrix memory from O(N^2) by 75% while preserving orientation traces.• Risk 2 (Over-smoothing in Severe c40 Quantization): If heavy c40 compression completely eliminates high frequencies, the HiFE module will be augmented with an auxiliary frequency loss term (L_freq) penalizing spectral divergence against c23 ground-truth features, actively enforcing high-frequency reconstruction.• Risk 3 (Cross-Dataset Landmark Alignment Failure): In the event of facial landmark detection failure on extreme poses in DFDC, a spatial bounding-box fallback mechanism utilizing YOLOv8-Face will be deployed to ensure zero dropped frames.
