## 1. Executive Summary of the Verified Project

### 1.1 Research Problem & Motivation
Spatial deepfake detectors (standard CNNs and Vision Transformers) suffer severe performance degradation when subjected to lossy post-processing, specifically JPEG compression commonly enforced by social media platforms (WhatsApp, X, Meta, Instagram). Conversely, global frequency transforms like the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) lack spatial localization and treat high-frequency GAN artifacts and JPEG block-boundary noise identically. 

This project explores a **Dual-Branch Spatio-Frequency Network** combining:
1. **Spatial Visual Context:** Deep semantic facial features extracted via an ImageNet-pretrained Xception backbone.
2. **Directional Wavelet-Packet Frequency Representations:** Multi-scale spectral sub-band energies extracted via 2D Wavelet Packet Decomposition (WPD) with a log-energy transformation.
3. **Adaptive Channel-Attention Fusion:** Feature gating dynamically recalibrating spatial vs. frequency streams.

### 1.2 Definitive Scientific Findings Summary
1. **Mathematical Correctness of GPU Wavelet Filter Bank:** The native PyTorch `DifferentiableWPD2D` layer is mathematically exact (passes 6/6 quadrature mirror and unit energy tests, CPU-CUDA error $< 4.76 \times 10^{-5}$, autograd gradient error vs. finite difference $< 4.82 \times 10^{-4}$), boosting end-to-end training throughput by **+45.9%** over sequential CPU PyWavelets.
2. **Wavelet Frequency Value Over Global Fourier:** Under identical controlled training (5,000 images, 2 epochs), Frequency-Only WPD achieves **0.7479 ROC-AUC**, whereas the 2D FFT Baseline fails catastrophically with **0.4554 ROC-AUC**. Global Fourier analysis without radial integration confuses structural low frequencies with forgery traces.
3. **Statistical Superiority of Gated Fusion Over Naive Concatenation:** Both `ParallelSEFusion` and `CrossGatedFusion` significantly outperform naive vector concatenation (`B3_naive_concat`) across all quality tiers ($p = 2.64 \times 10^{-6}$ and $p < 10^{-12}$; $\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.0033$).
4. **Frequency Fusion Improves ROC-AUC Over Spatial Alone:** `ParallelSEFusion` achieves statistically significant higher ROC-AUC than `B1_spatial_only` at all three tiers: $Q=100$ ($p = 2.99 \times 10^{-9}$), $Q=50$ ($p = 9.55 \times 10^{-12}$), and $Q=23$ ($p = 0.0250$).
5. **Reconciliation of Fusion Novelty:** `ParallelSEFusion` is empirically superior to `CrossGatedFusion` in representation ranking (ROC-AUC 0.9495 vs. 0.9382 at $Q=23$, $p < 10^{-12}$). `CrossGatedFusion` acts as a conservative regularizer that yields high precision (94.21%) and low FPR (4.27%) at default threshold $\tau=0.5$, but incurs a **30.55% False Negative Rate (missing 3,055 fake faces)**.
6. **Training Augmentation Requirement for Compression Resilience:** Frequency features require multi-epoch stochastic JPEG training augmentation to develop compression invariance. Under un-augmented 2-epoch budgets, spatial Xception achieved higher raw accuracy at $Q=23$ (87.06% vs. 84.69%) because un-augmented frequency branches inject JPEG quantization noise.

- **Strictly Paired Testing:** Exactly the same 20,000 test images are evaluated across all three quality tiers via deterministic on-the-fly JPEG compression, guaranteeing strictly paired sample comparisons for McNemar and ROC-AUC tests.

## 3. Definitive Architecture Description

### 3.1 Spatial Context Branch (`models/spatial_branch.py`)
- **Backbone Network:** `legacy_xception` (via PyTorch Image Models `timm`), initialized with ImageNet-1k pretrained weights.
- **Backbone Feature Map:** Extracts deepest feature tensor $X_{\text{spatial}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 2048 \times 8 \times 8}$.
- **Global Average Pooling (GAP):**
  $$\text{GAP}(X_{\text{spatial}})_c = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{w=1}^W X_{\text{spatial}}[c, h, w] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 2048}$$
- **Linear Projection:**
  $$F_{\text{spatial}} = \text{ReLU}\left( \text{BatchNorm1d}\left( W_{\text{proj\_s}} \cdot \text{GAP}(X_{\text{spatial}}) \right) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$

### 3.2 Directional Wavelet-Packet Frequency Branch (`models/wavelet_branch.py`)
- **Wavelet Basis:** Daubechies-4 (`db4`), orthogonal filter with 8 taps and 4 vanishing moments:
  $$h = [-0.010597, 0.032883, 0.030841, -0.187035, -0.027984, 0.630881, 0.714847, 0.230378]$$
  $$g[n] = (-1)^n h[7 - n]$$
- **2D Separable Filter Bank:** Quadrature mirror 2D filters $\mathbf{W}_{LL} = h \otimes h$, $\mathbf{W}_{LH} = h \otimes g$, $\mathbf{W}_{HL} = g \otimes h$, $\mathbf{W}_{HH} = g \otimes g$.
- **Decomposition Structure:** Full 2-level 2D Wavelet Packet Decomposition (WPD). Unlike standard DWT which only splits low-pass approximation bands, WPD recursively decomposes all approximation and detail bands, yielding $4^L = 4^2 = 16$ sub-bands:
  $$\mathcal{W} = \{ W_1, W_2, \dots, W_{16} \}, \quad W_i \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 64 \times 64}$$
- **Log-Energy Representation:** Normalizes exponential spectral falloff and stabilizes gradients:
  $$\tilde{W}_i = \log\left( |W_i| + \epsilon \right), \quad \epsilon = 10^{-8}$$
- **Lightweight Frequency CNN Stem:** Consumes the 16-channel tensor $\tilde{\mathcal{W}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 16 \times 64 \times 64}$:
  - Stage 1: $\text{Conv2D}(16 \to 64, k=3, s=2, p=1) \to \text{BN} \to \text{ReLU} \implies (B, 64, 32, 32)$
  - Stage 2: $\text{Conv2D}(64 \to 128, k=3, s=2, p=1) \to \text{BN} \to \text{ReLU} \implies (B, 128, 16, 16)$
  - Stage 3: $\text{Conv2D}(128 \to 256, k=3, s=2, p=1) \to \text{BN} \to \text{ReLU} \implies (B, 256, 8, 8)$
  - Spatial Pooling & Projection: $\text{GAP}(\text{Stage 3}) \implies (B, 256) \to \text{Linear}(256 \to 512) \to \text{BN} \to \text{ReLU} \implies F_{\text{frequency}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$.

### 3.3 Fusion Mechanisms (`models/fusion.py`)

#### Primary Architecture: `ParallelSEFusion` (Independent Squeeze-and-Excitation Gating)
Dynamically recalibrates spatial and frequency streams independently using channel attention:
$$\alpha_{\text{spatial}} = \sigma\left( W_{s2} \cdot \text{ReLU}(W_{s1} \cdot F_{\text{spatial}}) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$
$$\alpha_{\text{frequency}} = \sigma\left( W_{f2} \cdot \text{ReLU}(W_{f1} \cdot F_{\text{frequency}}) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$
$$\hat{F}_{\text{spatial}} = F_{\text{spatial}} \odot \alpha_{\text{spatial}}, \quad \hat{F}_{\text{frequency}} = F_{\text{frequency}} \odot \alpha_{\text{frequency}}$$
$$F_{\text{fused}} = \text{Dropout}_{0.3}\left( \text{ReLU}\left( \text{BatchNorm1d}\left( W_{\text{fuse}} \cdot [\hat{F}_{\text{spatial}} \,\|\, \hat{F}_{\text{frequency}}] \right) \right) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$
where reduction ratio $r=8$, $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 512}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 64}$.

#### Regularized Variant: `CrossGatedFusion` (Cross-Conditioned Gating)
Computes a joint concatenated representation to generate cross-modality gating vectors:
$$F_{\text{joint}} = [F_{\text{spatial}} \,\|\, F_{\text{frequency}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1024}$$
$$G = \text{ReLU}\left( \text{BatchNorm1d}\left( W_{g1} \cdot F_{\text{joint}} \right) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256}$$
$$g_{\text{spatial}} = \sigma\left( W_{gs} \cdot G \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}, \quad g_{\text{frequency}} = \sigma\left( W_{gf} \cdot G \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$
$$F_{\text{fused}} = \text{Dropout}_{0.3}\left( \text{ReLU}\left( \text{BatchNorm1d}\left( W_{\text{cross}} \cdot [(F_{\text{spatial}} \odot g_{\text{spatial}}) \,\|\, (F_{\text{frequency}} \odot g_{\text{frequency}})] \right) \right) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}$$

### 3.4 Classification Head & Objective Function
$$\hat{y} = W_{\text{cls}} \cdot \text{Dropout}_{0.5}(F_{\text{fused}}) + b_{\text{cls}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1}$$
$$P(\text{Fake} \mid I) = \sigma(\hat{y}) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\hat{y})}$$
Optimized via Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ y_i \log(\sigma(\hat{y}_i)) + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - \sigma(\hat{y}_i)) \right]$$

### 6.4 The Q=23 Trade-Off Investigation
At $Q=23$, `CrossGatedFusion` achieves **94.21% Precision** and a **4.27% FPR**, but its accuracy drops to **82.59%**.
- **Underlying Cause:** `CrossGatedFusion` experiences severe probability shrinkage toward zero (mean predicted fake probability drops to 0.3541).
- At $\tau=0.5$, it acts as an ultra-conservative detector: it correctly rejects 95.73% of authentic faces, but produces **3,055 False Negatives (30.55% FNR)**.
- Conversely, `ParallelSEFusion` catches 95.01% of fakes ($	ext{FN}=499$), but misclassifies 2,563 authentic faces ($	ext{FPR}=25.63\%$).
- **Threshold Calibration (Youden's $J$ Optimization):**
  - CrossGated optimal threshold $\tau^* = 0.2240 \implies \text{Accuracy rises from } 82.59\% \to \mathbf{86.55\%}$ (Recall rises to 88.14%).
  - ParallelSE optimal threshold $\tau^* = 0.8169 \implies \text{Accuracy rises from } 84.69\% \to \mathbf{87.71\%}$ (FPR drops to 11.24%).

## 15. Unresolved Scientific Questions and Limitations

### 15.1 The 3 Primary Scientific Limitations
1. **Absence of Empirical Cross-Generator Transfer Data:** The evaluation is restricted to StyleGAN2 vs. FFHQ. Zero-shot transfer to alias-free GANs (StyleGAN3) or latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, FLUX) has not been measured.
2. **Dependence of Frequency Forensics on Data Augmentation:** Multi-level wavelet packet sub-bands are vulnerable to $8 \times 8$ JPEG block boundary noise unless explicitly exposed to stochastic JPEG compression during training. Under restricted training budgets without compression augmentation, spatial Xception remains more resilient to severe quantization than dual-branch models.
3. **Trade-off Between False Alarm Suppression and Fake Recall:** Under heavy compression ($Q=23$), `ParallelSEFusion` catches 95.01% of fakes at the expense of a 25.63% False Positive Rate, whereas `CrossGatedFusion` achieves a 4.27% False Positive Rate at the expense of missing 30.55% of synthetic faces. A single operating threshold cannot simultaneously optimize both forensic objectives under severe degradation.

### 15.2 Mandatory Authorship Recommendations
- **Primary Architecture:** Present `ParallelSEFusion` as the proposed primary model architecture (**"Dual-Branch Spatio-Frequency Network with Parallel Adaptive Channel Recalibration"**).
- **Secondary Exploration:** Present `CrossGatedFusion` as an alternative conservative gating mechanism optimized specifically for high-stakes judicial applications where false accusations must be minimized.
- **Scientific Honesty:** Clearly state that all controlled baseline models were trained for 2 epochs on 5,000 images, and explicitly distinguish their metrics from the full-scale 8-epoch model trained on 100,000 images.
